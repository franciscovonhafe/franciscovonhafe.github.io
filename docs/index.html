Francisco von Hafe

Economist at Value for Health CoLABPhD Candidate in Economics at Nova School of Business and Economics

I am an applied microeconomist working in health economics. I study how economic incentives, stakeholder priorities and market mechanisms shape healthcare decisions, the delivery of care, and the adoption of new health technologies, including digital health.

At Value for Health CoLAB (VOH.CoLAB), I work on applied projects in economic evaluation, cost analysis, reimbursement and the implementation of digital health services. My doctoral research at Nova SBE combines econometric analysis, systematic evidence synthesis and stakeholder-based methods. I am also a researcher at the Comprehensive Health Research Center (CHRC), NOVA Medical School.

  • 2026

    Beyond the app: a marketness assessment of digital health, sports, and well-being – a systematic review

    von Hafe, F., Santos, A. R., Londral, A. R., Silva, A. M., Lopes, J., Carvalho, N., Ferreira, S., & Azevedo, S.

    Frontiers in Digital Health, 8, 1871114

    Digital healthMarkets & incentives
    DOI 10.3389/fdgth.2026.1871114
    Summary

    Digital health, sports and well-being solutions often fail to sustain use, even when they are effective. This systematic review applies a marketness framework, which assesses how fully a market is constituted against four criteria, to 17 empirical studies grouped into condition management, lifestyle and behaviour, and physical activity solutions. None of the three categories met all four criteria, suggesting that investment, regulatory and participation decisions are often made without a clear picture of the underlying market conditions.

  • 2026

    What to measure for monitoring and decision-making in a case management intervention for emergency department high-users: different stakeholder priorities

    von Hafe, F., Loureiro, D., Azevedo, S., Januário, A., Lourenço, M., Mesquita, A., Guede-Fernández, F., & Londral, A. R.

    BMC Health Services Research

    Healthcare deliveryEconomic evaluation
    DOI 10.1186/s12913-026-15460-8
    Summary

    Evaluations of case management for frequent emergency department users usually take the provider’s perspective and rely on hospital utilisation indicators. Using participatory action research in a Portuguese programme (GRHU), we defined 22 indicators and found that board members, emergency department leadership and programme managers prioritised different ones, while only nine could be fully measured with routine hospital data. For the highest-ranked measurable outcome, emergency department visits, the observed one-year reduction in visit costs exceeded delivery costs in a before-after comparison (ratio of 1.22).

  • 2026

    Mapping methodologies for economic evaluation of digital health technologies: a scoping review

    Santos, A. R., von Hafe, F., Sampaio, F., Londral, A. R., & Perelman, J.

    Value in Health, 29(2), 311–323

    Economic evaluationDigital health
    DOI 10.1016/j.jval.2025.09.012
    Summary

    Digital health technologies challenge conventional economic evaluation because of their diverse functions, evolving nature and indirect impact pathways. This scoping review maps the methodological frameworks proposed for their economic evaluation across study design, comparators, time horizon, perspective, effectiveness measures, cost components and uncertainty analysis. It finds substantial variation, including limited attention to productivity costs, and calls for flexible, lifecycle-aligned evaluation models and standardised guidance.

  • 2021

    Formal home care use and spousal health outcomes

    Gonçalves, J., von Hafe, F., & Filipe, L.

    Social Science & Medicine, 287, 114373

    Health outcomes & public health
    DOI 10.1016/j.socscimed.2021.114373 Accepted manuscript (PDF)
    Summary

    Using data from the Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe (SHARE), this study matches new users of formal home care with non-users to examine how paid professional care relates to the health of the user’s spouse. After accounting for baseline differences and potential confounding changes, it concludes that formal home care is unlikely to affect spousal physical or mental health in the short run.
